**Bhimrao Ramji Ambedkar**[[a]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-1) (14 April 1891– 6 December 1956) was an Indian jurist, economist, social reformer and politician who chaired the committee that drafted the [Constitution of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_India "Constitution of India") based on the debates of the [Constituent Assembly of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constituent_Assembly_of_India "Constituent Assembly of India") and the first draft of Sir [Benegal Narsing Rau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benegal_Narsing_Rau "Benegal Narsing Rau").[[1]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-duara-rau-2018-lead-2)[[2]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-elangovan_bnrau-3)[[3]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-cewing-rau-rights-2020-lead1-4)[[4]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-prasse-freeman-2023-lead-5)[[5]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-britannica-bnrau-6) Ambedkar served as [Law and Justice minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Law_and_Justice_(India) "Ministry of Law and Justice (India)") in the [first cabinet of Jawaharlal Nehru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Nehru_ministry "First Nehru ministry"). He later renounced [Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism "Hinduism") and converted to [Buddhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhism "Buddhism"), inspiring the [Dalit Buddhist movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dalit_Buddhist_movement "Dalit Buddhist movement").[[6]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-britannica-ambedkar-7)

After graduating from [Elphinstone College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elphinstone_College "Elphinstone College"), [University of Bombay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bombay "University of Bombay"), Ambedkar studied economics at [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University "Columbia University") and the [London School of Economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_School_of_Economics "London School of Economics"), receiving doctorates in 1927 and 1923, respectively, and was among a handful of Indian students to have done so at either institution in the 1920s. During his time at Columbia University, Ambedkar came under the influence of [John Dewey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Dewey "John Dewey") and his philosophy of [pragmatism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pragmatism "Pragmatism").[[7]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-epw.in-8)[[8]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-Skaria_450%E2%80%93465-9)[[9]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-krishnamurty-ambedkar-education-10) He also trained in the law at [Gray's Inn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gray's_Inn "Gray's Inn"), London. In his early career, he was an economist, professor, and lawyer. His later life was marked by his political activities; he became involved in campaigning and negotiations for [partition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_of_India "Partition of India"), publishing journals, advocating political rights and social freedom for [Dalits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dalit "Dalit"), and contributing to the establishment of the state of India. In 1956, he converted to [Buddhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhism "Buddhism"), initiating mass conversions of Dalits.[[10]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-11)

In 1990, the [Bharat Ratna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bharat_Ratna "Bharat Ratna"), India's highest civilian award, was posthumously conferred on Ambedkar. The salutation _[Jai Bhim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jai\_Bhim "Jai Bhim")_ ([lit.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literary_translation "Literary translation") "Hail Bhim") is used by his followers to honour him. He is also referred to by the honorific [Babasaheb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babasaheb_(title) "Babasaheb (title)"), meaning "Respected Father".

## Early life and education

Ambedkar was born on 14 April 1891 in the town and military cantonment of [Mhow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mhow "Mhow") (now officially known as Dr Ambedkar Nagar, [Madhya Pradesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madhya_Pradesh "Madhya Pradesh")).[[11]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-12) He was the 14th and last child of [Ramji Maloji Sakpal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramji_Sakpal "Ramji Sakpal"), an army officer who held the rank of [Subedar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subedar "Subedar"), and Bhimabai Sakpal, daughter of Laxman Murbadkar.[[12]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-Columbia-13) His [family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambedkar_family "Ambedkar family") was of [Marathi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marathi_people "Marathi people") background from the town of [Ambadawe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambadawe "Ambadawe") ([Mandangad taluka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandangad "Mandangad")) in [Ratnagiri district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ratnagiri_district "Ratnagiri district") of modern-day [Maharashtra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maharashtra "Maharashtra"). Ambedkar's ancestors had long worked for the [army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_armies "Presidency armies") of the [British East India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_India_Company "East India Company"), and his father served in the [British Indian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Indian_Army "British Indian Army") at the Mhow cantonment.[[13]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-14)

Ambedkar was born into a [Mahar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahar "Mahar") (Dalit) caste, who were treated as [untouchables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Untouchability "Untouchability") and subjected to socio-economic discrimination.[[14]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-15) Although they attended school, Ambedkar and other untouchable children were segregated and given little attention or help by teachers. They were not allowed to sit inside the class. When they needed to drink water, someone from a higher caste had to pour that water from a height as they were not allowed to touch either the water or the vessel that contained it. This task was usually performed for the young Ambedkar by the school [peon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peon "Peon"), and if the peon was not available then he had to go without water; he described the situation later in his writings as _"No peon, No Water"_.[[15]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-Waiting_for_Visa-16) He was required to sit on a [gunny sack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gunny_sack "Gunny sack") which he had to take home with him.[[16]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-17)

Ramji Sakpal retired in 1894 and the family moved to [Satara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satara_district "Satara district") two years later. Shortly after their move, Ambedkar's mother died. The children were cared for by their paternal aunt and lived in difficult circumstances. Three sons– Balaram, Anandrao and Bhimrao– and two daughters– Manjula and Tulasa– of the Ambedkars survived them. Of his brothers and sisters, only Ambedkar passed his examinations and went to high school. His original surname was _Sakpal_ but his father registered his name as _Ambadawekar_ in school, meaning he comes from his native village '[Ambadawe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambadawe "Ambadawe")' in Ratnagiri district.[[17]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-loksatta.com-18)[[18]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-19)[[19]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-m.divyamarathi.bhaskar.com-20)[[20]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-Ambavadekar-21) His [Marathi Brahmin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marathi_Brahmin "Marathi Brahmin") teacher, Krishnaji Keshav Ambedkar, changed his surname from 'Ambadawekar' to his own surname 'Ambedkar' in school records.[[21]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-22)[[22]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-23)[[23]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-24)[[24]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-25)[[25]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-26)

### Education

[![Image 3](http://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f8/Young_Ambedkar.gif/250px-Young_Ambedkar.gif?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Young_Ambedkar.gif)

Ambedkar as a student

In 1897, Ambedkar's family moved to Mumbai where Ambedkar became the only untouchable enrolled at [Elphinstone High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elphinstone_High_School "Elphinstone High School"). In 1906, when he was about 15 years old, he married a nine-year-old girl, Ramabai. The match was [arranged](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arranged_marriage_in_the_Indian_subcontinent "Arranged marriage in the Indian subcontinent") by the couple's parents, in accordance with prevailing custom at that time.[[26]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-27) In 1912, Ramabai gave birth to their first child, [Yashwant Ambedkar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yashwant_Ambedkar "Yashwant Ambedkar"), at the age of 14.[[27]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-28)[[28]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-29)

In 1907, he passed his matriculation examination and in the following year he entered [Elphinstone College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elphinstone_College "Elphinstone College"), which was affiliated to the [University of Bombay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bombay "University of Bombay"), becoming, according to him, the first from his Mahar caste to do so. When he passed his English fourth standard examinations, the people of his community wanted to celebrate because they considered that he had reached "great heights" which he says was "hardly an occasion compared to the state of education in other communities". A public ceremony was evoked, to celebrate his success, by the community, and it was at this occasion that he was presented with a biography of the [Buddha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gautama_Buddha "Gautama Buddha") by Dada Keluskar, the author and a family friend.[[29]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-30)

[![Image 4](http://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/32/Dr._Babasaheb_Ambedkar_with_wife_Ramabai_Ambedkar.jpg/330px-Dr._Babasaheb_Ambedkar_with_wife_Ramabai_Ambedkar.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Dr._Babasaheb_Ambedkar_with_wife_Ramabai_Ambedkar.jpg)

In 1907, Dr. B.R. Ambedkar married his first wife, 9 year old [Ramabai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramabai_Ambedkar "Ramabai Ambedkar"), in accordance with prevailing customs.

By 1912, he obtained his degree in economics and political science from Bombay University, and prepared to take up employment with the Baroda state government. His wife had just moved his young family and started work when he had to quickly return to Mumbai to see his ailing father, who died on 2 February 1913.[[30]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-Columbia3-31)

[![Image 5](http://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/34/Dr._Babasaheb_Ambedkar_in_Columbia_University.jpg/250px-Dr._Babasaheb_Ambedkar_in_Columbia_University.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Dr._Babasaheb_Ambedkar_in_Columbia_University.jpg)

Ambedkar at [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University "Columbia University"), c.1916

In 1913, at the age of 22, Ambedkar was awarded a Baroda State Scholarship of £11.50 (Sterling) per month for three years under a scheme established by [Sayajirao Gaekwad III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sayajirao_Gaekwad_III "Sayajirao Gaekwad III") ([Gaekwad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaekwad "Gaekwad") of [Baroda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princely_State_of_Baroda "Princely State of Baroda")) that was designed to provide opportunities for postgraduate education at [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University "Columbia University") in [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City "New York City"). Soon after arriving there he settled in rooms at [Livingston Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wallach_Hall "Wallach Hall") with Naval Bhathena, a [Parsi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parsi "Parsi") who was to be a lifelong friend. He passed his M.A. exam in June 1915, majoring in economics, and other subjects of Sociology, History, Philosophy and Anthropology. He presented a thesis, _Ancient Indian Commerce_. Ambedkar was influenced by [John Dewey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Dewey "John Dewey") and his work on democracy.[[31]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-32) In 1916, he completed his second master's thesis, _National Dividend of India – A Historic and Analytical Study_, for a second M.A.[[32]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-33) On 9 May, he presented the paper _[Castes in India: Their Mechanism, Genesis and Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castes\_in\_India:\_Their\_Mechanism,\_Genesis\_and\_Development "Castes in India: Their Mechanism, Genesis and Development")_ before a seminar conducted by the anthropologist [Alexander Goldenweiser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Goldenweiser_(anthropologist) "Alexander Goldenweiser (anthropologist)"). Ambedkar received his Ph.D. degree in economics at Columbia in 1927.[[9]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-krishnamurty-ambedkar-education-10)

[![Image 6](http://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/31/Dr._B._R._Ambedkar_with_his_professors_and_friends_from_the_London_School_of_Economics_and_Political_Science%2C_1916-17.jpg/250px-Dr._B._R._Ambedkar_with_his_professors_and_friends_from_the_London_School_of_Economics_and_Political_Science%2C_1916-17.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Dr._B._R._Ambedkar_with_his_professors_and_friends_from_the_London_School_of_Economics_and_Political_Science,_1916-17.jpg)

Ambedkar (In center line, first from right) with his professors and friends from the London School of Economics (1916–17)

In October 1916, he enrolled for the [Bar course](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bar_examination "Bar examination") at [Gray's Inn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gray's_Inn "Gray's Inn"), and at the same time enrolled at the [London School of Economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_School_of_Economics "London School of Economics") where he started working on a doctoral thesis. In June 1917, he returned to India because his scholarship from Baroda ended. His book collection was dispatched on a different ship from the one he was on, and that ship was torpedoed and sunk by a German submarine.[[30]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-Columbia3-31) He got permission to return to London to submit his thesis within four years. He returned at the first opportunity, and completed a master's degree in 1921. His thesis was on "The problem of the rupee: Its origin and its solution".[[33]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-firstpost.com-34) In 1923, he completed a D.Sc. in Economics which was awarded from [University of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_London "University of London"), and the same year he was called to the Bar by Gray's Inn.[[9]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-krishnamurty-ambedkar-education-10)

## Opposition to untouchability

[![Image 7](http://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/d/d7/Ambedkar_Barrister.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail_unscaled)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Ambedkar_Barrister.jpg)

Ambedkar as a barrister in 1922

As Ambedkar was educated by the [Princely State of Baroda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princely_State_of_Baroda "Princely State of Baroda"), he was bound to serve it. He was appointed Military Secretary to the Gaikwad but had to quit in a short time. He described the incident in his autobiography, _[Waiting for a Visa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waiting\_for\_a\_Visa "Waiting for a Visa")_.[[34]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-Rewriting_for_Visa-35) Thereafter, he tried to find ways to make a living for his growing family. He worked as a private tutor, as an accountant, and established an investment consulting business, but it failed when his clients learned that he was an untouchable.[[35]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-36) In 1918, he became professor of political economy in the [Sydenham College of Commerce and Economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sydenham_College "Sydenham College") in Mumbai. Although he was successful with the students, other professors objected to his sharing a drinking-water jug with them.[[36]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-37)

Ambedkar had been invited to testify before the [Southborough Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southborough_Committee "Southborough Committee"), which was preparing the [Government of India Act 1919](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_India_Act_1919 "Government of India Act 1919"). At this hearing, Ambedkar argued for creating separate [electorates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_district "Electoral district") and [reservations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reservation_in_India "Reservation in India") for untouchables and other religious communities.[[37]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-Tejani-38) In 1920, he began the publication of the weekly _[Mooknayak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mooknayak "Mooknayak")_ (Leader of the Silent) in Mumbai with the help of [Shahu of Kolhapur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shahu_of_Kolhapur "Shahu of Kolhapur"), that is, Shahu IV (1874–1922).[[38]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-Jaffrelot-39)

Ambedkar went on to work as a legal professional. In 1926, he successfully defended three non-Brahmin leaders who had accused the Brahmin community of ruining India and were then subsequently sued for libel. [Dhananjay Keer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhananjay_Keer "Dhananjay Keer") notes, "The victory was resounding, both socially and individually, for the clients and the doctor".[[39]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-40)

[![Image 8](http://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/ab/Dr._Babasaheb_Ambedkar_being_sworn_in_as_independent_India%E2%80%99s_first_Law_Minister_by_President_Dr._Rajendra_Prasad%2C_Prime_Minister_Jawaharlal_Nehru_looks_on_May_8%2C_1950.jpg/250px-thumbnail.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Dr._Babasaheb_Ambedkar_being_sworn_in_as_independent_India%E2%80%99s_first_Law_Minister_by_President_Dr._Rajendra_Prasad,_Prime_Minister_Jawaharlal_Nehru_looks_on_May_8,_1950.jpg)

Sworn in as India's first Law and Justice Minister

While practising law in the [Bombay High Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombay_High_Court "Bombay High Court"), he tried to promote education to untouchables and uplift them. His first organised attempt was his establishment of the central institution [Bahishkrit Hitakarini Sabha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahishkrit_Hitakarini_Sabha "Bahishkrit Hitakarini Sabha"), intended to promote education and socio-economic improvement, as well as the welfare of "[outcastes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dalit "Dalit")", at the time referred to as depressed classes.[[40]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-41) For the defence of Dalit rights, he started many periodicals like _Mook Nayak_, _Bahishkrit Bharat_, and _Equality Janta_.[[41]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-42)

He was appointed to the Bombay Presidency Committee to work with the all-European [Simon Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_Commission "Simon Commission") in 1925.[[42]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-43) This commission had sparked great protests across India, and while its report was ignored by most Indians, Ambedkar himself wrote a separate set of recommendations for the future Constitution of India.[[43]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-44)

By 1927, Ambedkar had decided to launch active movements against [untouchability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Untouchability "Untouchability"). He began with public movements and marches to open up public drinking water resources. He also began a struggle for the right to enter Hindu temples. He led _[a satyagraha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahad\_Satyagraha "Mahad Satyagraha")_ in [Mahad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahad "Mahad") to fight for the right of the untouchable community to draw water from the main water tank of the town.[[44]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-45) In a conference in late 1927, Ambedkar publicly condemned the classic Hindu text, the [Manusmriti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manusmriti "Manusmriti") (Laws of Manu), for ideologically justifying caste discrimination and "untouchability", and he ceremonially burned copies of the ancient text. On 25 December 1927, he led thousands of followers to burn copies of Manusmriti.[[45]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-46)[[46]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-47) Thus annually 25 December is celebrated as _Manusmriti Dahan Din_ (Manusmriti Burning Day) by [Ambedkarites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dalit_Buddhist_movement "Dalit Buddhist movement") and [Dalits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dalit "Dalit").[[47]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-Menon_2014-48)[[48]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-49)

In 1930, Ambedkar launched the [Kalaram Temple movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalaram_Temple "Kalaram Temple") after three months of preparation. About 15,000 volunteers assembled at [Kalaram Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalaram_Temple "Kalaram Temple") satygraha making one of the greatest processions of [Nashik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nashik "Nashik"). The procession was headed by a military band and a batch of scouts; women and men walked with discipline, order and determination to see the god for the first time. When they reached the gates, the gates were closed by Brahmin authorities.[[49]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-keer-50)

## Poona Pact

[![Image 9](http://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/16/M.R._Jayakar%2C_Tej_Bahadur_Sapru_and_Dr._Babasaheb_Ambedkar_at_Yerwada_jail%2C_in_Poona%2C_on_24_September_1932%2C_the_day_the_Poona_Pact_was_signed.jpg/250px-M.R._Jayakar%2C_Tej_Bahadur_Sapru_and_Dr._Babasaheb_Ambedkar_at_Yerwada_jail%2C_in_Poona%2C_on_24_September_1932%2C_the_day_the_Poona_Pact_was_signed.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:M.R._Jayakar,_Tej_Bahadur_Sapru_and_Dr._Babasaheb_Ambedkar_at_Yerwada_jail,_in_Poona,_on_24_September_1932,_the_day_the_Poona_Pact_was_signed.jpg)

M.R. Jayakar, Tej Bahadur Sapru and Ambedkar at Yerwada jail, in Poona, on 24 September 1932, the day the Poona Pact was signed

In 1932, the British colonial government announced the formation of a separate electorate for "Depressed Classes" in the [Communal Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communal_Award "Communal Award"). [Mahatma Gandhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahatma_Gandhi "Mahatma Gandhi") fiercely opposed a separate electorate for untouchables, saying he feared that such an arrangement would divide the Hindu community.[[50]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-51)[[51]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-52)[[52]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-53) Gandhi protested by fasting while imprisoned in the [Yerwada Central Jail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yerwada_Central_Jail "Yerwada Central Jail") of [Poona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poona "Poona"). Following the fast, congressional politicians and activists such as [Madan Mohan Malaviya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madan_Mohan_Malaviya "Madan Mohan Malaviya") and [Palwankar Baloo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palwankar_Baloo "Palwankar Baloo") organised joint meetings with Ambedkar and his supporters at Yerwada.[[53]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-54) On 25 September 1932, the agreement, known as the [Poona Pact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poona_Pact "Poona Pact") was signed between Ambedkar (on behalf of the depressed classes among Hindus) and [Madan Mohan Malaviya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madan_Mohan_Malaviya "Madan Mohan Malaviya") (on behalf of the other Hindus). The agreement gave reserved seats for the depressed classes in the Provisional legislatures within the general electorate. Due to the pact the depressed class received 148 seats in the legislature instead of the 71, as allocated in the [Communal Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communal_Award "Communal Award") proposed earlier by the colonial government under [Prime Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_the_United_Kingdom "Prime Minister of the United Kingdom")[Ramsay MacDonald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramsay_MacDonald "Ramsay MacDonald"). The text used the term "Depressed Classes" to denote Untouchables among Hindus who were later called Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes under the India Act 1935, and the later Indian Constitution of 1950.[[54]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-55) In the Poona Pact, a unified electorate was in principle formed, but primary and secondary elections allowed Untouchables in practice to choose their own candidates.[[55]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-56)

## Political career

[![Image 10](http://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c7/Rajagriha%2C_Bombay%2C_February_1934._%28L_to_R%29_Yashwant%2C_BR_Ambedkar%2C_Ramabai%2C_Laxmibai%2C_Mukundrao%2C_and_Tobby.jpg/250px-Rajagriha%2C_Bombay%2C_February_1934._%28L_to_R%29_Yashwant%2C_BR_Ambedkar%2C_Ramabai%2C_Laxmibai%2C_Mukundrao%2C_and_Tobby.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Rajagriha,_Bombay,_February_1934._(L_to_R)_Yashwant,_BR_Ambedkar,_Ramabai,_Laxmibai,_Mukundrao,_and_Tobby.jpg)

Ambedkar with his family members at Rajgraha in February 1934. From left – Yashwant (son), Ambedkar, Ramabai (wife), Laxmibai (wife of his elder brother, Balaram), Mukund (nephew) and Ambedkar's favourite dog, Tobby

[![Image 11](http://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f4/Dr._Babasaheb_Ambedkar_is_addressing_a_seminar_organized_by_the_National_Sports_Club_of_India%2C_New_Delhi_and_Columbia_University%2C_USA_on_30_October_1954_%28cropped%29.jpg/250px-thumbnail.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Dr._Babasaheb_Ambedkar_is_addressing_a_seminar_organized_by_the_National_Sports_Club_of_India,_New_Delhi_and_Columbia_University,_USA_on_30_October_1954_(cropped).jpg)

Ambedkar addresses a seminar in New Delhi on the occasion of the [Columbia University Bicentennial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University_Bicentennial "Columbia University Bicentennial"), 1954

In 1935, Ambedkar was appointed principal of the [Government Law College, Bombay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_Law_College,_Bombay "Government Law College, Bombay"), a position he held for two years. He also served as the chairman of Governing body of [Ramjas College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramjas_College "Ramjas College"), [University of Delhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delhi_University "Delhi University"), after the death of its Founder Shri Rai Kedarnath.[[56]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-57) Settling in Bombay (today called Mumbai), Ambedkar oversaw the construction of a house, and stocked his personal library with more than 50,000 books.[[57]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-Columbia5-58) His wife [Ramabai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramabai_Bhimrao_Ambedkar "Ramabai Bhimrao Ambedkar") died after a long illness the same year. It had been her long-standing wish to go on a pilgrimage to [Pandharpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pandharpur "Pandharpur"), but Ambedkar had refused to let her go, telling her that he would create a new Pandharpur for her instead of Hinduism's Pandharpur which treated them as untouchables. At the Yeola Conversion Conference on 13 October in Nasik, Ambedkar announced his intention to convert to a different religion and exhorted his followers to leave [Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism "Hinduism").[[57]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-Columbia5-58) He would repeat his message at many public meetings across India.

In 1936, Ambedkar founded the [Independent Labour Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_Labour_Party_(India) "Independent Labour Party (India)"), which contested the 1937 Bombay election to the [Central Legislative Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Legislative_Assembly "Central Legislative Assembly") for the 13 reserved and 4 general seats, and secured 11 and 3 seats respectively.[[58]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-Jaffrelot_2005_76%E2%80%9377-59)

Ambedkar published his book _[Annihilation of Caste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annihilation\_of\_Caste "Annihilation of Caste")_ on 15 May 1936.[[59]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-60) It strongly criticised Hindu orthodox religious leaders and the caste system in general,[[60]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-Mungekar-61)[[61]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-62) and included "a rebuke of Gandhi" on the subject.[[62]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-NYT01-63) Later, in a 1955 BBC interview, he accused Gandhi of writing in opposition of the caste system in English language papers while writing in support of it in Gujarati language papers.[[63]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-64) In his writings, Ambedkar also accused [Jawaharlal Nehru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jawaharlal_Nehru "Jawaharlal Nehru") of being "conscious of the fact that he is a [Brahmin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahmin "Brahmin")".[[64]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-65)

During this time, Ambedkar also fought against the _khoti_ system prevalent in [Konkan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konkan "Konkan"), where _khots_, or government revenue collectors, regularly exploited farmers and tenants. In 1937, Ambedkar tabled a bill in the Bombay Legislative Assembly aimed at abolishing the _khoti_ system by creating a direct relationship between government and farmers.[[65]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-66)

Ambedkar served on the Defence Advisory Committee[[66]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-autogenerated2-67) and the [Viceroy's Executive Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viceroy's_Executive_Council "Viceroy's Executive Council") as [minister of labour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Labour_and_Employment_(India) "Ministry of Labour and Employment (India)").[[66]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-autogenerated2-67) Before the [Day of Deliverance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Day_of_Deliverance_(India) "Day of Deliverance (India)") events, Ambedkar stated that he was interested in participating: "I read Mr. Jinnah's statement and I felt ashamed to have allowed him to steal a march over me and rob me of the language and the sentiment which I, more than Mr. Jinnah, was entitled to use." He went on to suggest that the communities he worked with were twenty times more oppressed by Congress policies than were Indian Muslims; he clarified that he was criticizing Congress, and not all Hindus.[[67]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-dr-ambedkar-68) Jinnah and Ambedkar jointly addressed the heavily attended Day of Deliverance event in [Bhindi Bazaar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhindi_Bazaar "Bhindi Bazaar"), [Bombay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mumbai "Mumbai"), where both expressed "fiery" criticisms of the Congress party, and according to one observer, suggested that Islam and Hinduism were irreconcilable.[[67]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-dr-ambedkar-68)[[68]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-man-who-divided-india-69)

[![Image 12](http://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c3/Rajgruh%2C_the_residence_of_Babasaheb_Ambedkar_in_Dadar%2C_Mumbai.jpg/250px-Rajgruh%2C_the_residence_of_Babasaheb_Ambedkar_in_Dadar%2C_Mumbai.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Rajgruh,_the_residence_of_Babasaheb_Ambedkar_in_Dadar,_Mumbai.jpg)

Following his success as a barrister, Ambedkar built a house in Dadar, a Hindu colony of Mumbai.

In his work _[Who Were the Shudras?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Who\_Were\_the\_Shudras%3F "Who Were the Shudras?")_, Ambedkar tried to explain the formation of untouchables. He saw Shudras and Ati Shudras who form the lowest caste in the ritual hierarchy of the [caste system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caste_system_in_India "Caste system in India"), as separate from Untouchables. Ambedkar oversaw the transformation of his political party into the [Scheduled Castes Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scheduled_Castes_Federation "Scheduled Castes Federation"). It did not fare well in the 1946 provincial elections, but in Bengal, it managed to elect Ambedkar to the [Constituent Assembly of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constituent_Assembly_of_India "Constituent Assembly of India") by winning support from Congress legislators.[[69]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-70)[[70]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-71)

[Jagjivan Ram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jagjivan_Ram "Jagjivan Ram")'s wife Indrani Jagjivan Ram wrote in her memoir that Ambedkar persuaded her husband to ask [Mahatma Gandhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahatma_Gandhi "Mahatma Gandhi") for his inclusion in Nehru's cabinet in independent India. Initially, Jagjivan Ram consulted [Vallabhbhai Patel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vallabhbhai_Patel "Vallabhbhai Patel") before asking Gandhi to recommend Ambedkar to Nehru for inclusion in cabinet, adding that Ambedkar had "given up his antagonism to Congress and Gandhiji". Ambedkar was ultimately included as the [law minister of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_minister_of_India "Law minister of India") in the [First Nehru ministry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Nehru_ministry "First Nehru ministry") after Gandhi recommended his name to Nehru.[[71]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-72)[[72]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-73)

On 27 September 1951, Ambedkar resigned from Nehru's cabinet ministry after [Hindu code bill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu_code_bill "Hindu code bill") was defeated in parliament.[[73]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-Hande_Ambedkar_2009_p.-74)

Ambedkar contested in the Bombay North first Indian General Election of 1952, but lost to his former assistant and Congress Party candidate [Narayan Sadoba Kajrolkar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narayan_Sadoba_Kajrolkar "Narayan Sadoba Kajrolkar"). Ambedkar became a member of [Rajya Sabha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajya_Sabha "Rajya Sabha"), probably an appointed member. He tried to enter [Lok Sabha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lok_Sabha "Lok Sabha") again in the by-election of 1954 from Bhandara, but he placed third (the Congress Party won). By the time of the second general election in 1957, Ambedkar had died.

Ambedkar also criticised Islamic practice in South Asia. While justifying the [Partition of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_of_India "Partition of India"), he condemned child marriage and the mistreatment of women in Muslim society.

> No words can adequately express the great and many evils of polygamy and concubinage, and especially as a source of misery to a Muslim woman. Take the caste system. Everybody infers that Islam must be free from slavery and caste. [...] [While slavery existed], much of its support was derived from Islam and Islamic countries. While the prescriptions by the Prophet regarding the just and humane treatment of slaves contained in the Koran are praiseworthy, there is nothing whatever in Islam that lends support to the abolition of this curse. But if slavery has gone, caste among Musalmans [Muslims] has remained.[[74]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-Ambedkar-75)

## On partition

After the [Lahore resolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahore_resolution "Lahore resolution") (1940) of the Muslim League demanding a separate state, Ambedkar published _Pakistan or The Partition of India_ (1940) in which sought to define the ILP's stance, and _Thoughts on Pakistan_ (1941). In these, Ambedkar argued that attempts to forge Hindu-Muslim unity within a single state would lead to continuous conflict, administrative chaos, and potential civil war. He dismissed as simplistic the complaint that the British were to blame for the communal divide: a "policy of divide and rule, allowing that the British do resort to it, cannot succeed unless there are elements which make division possible, and further if the policy succeeds for such a long time, it means that the elements which divide are more or less permanent and irreconcilable and are not transitory or superficial"._[[75]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B.\_R.\_Ambedkar#cite\_note-:42-76):328_

"In the last analysis"—Ambedkar quotes the Irish republican, [Sinn Féin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinn_F%C3%A9in "Sinn Féin") Vice-President, [Michael O'Flanagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_O'Flanagan "Michael O'Flanagan")— "the test of nationality is the wish of the people". If the Muslim majority in the north-west provinces and east Bengal insisted on political separation, then—as O'Flanagen conceded in the case of [Protestant resistance in Ulster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_of_Ireland "Partition of Ireland") to an all-Ireland republic—there was no nationalist case for a single constitution.[[75]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-:42-76):367 Failing to confront this reality, Gandhi and others within the Hindu leadership of Congress had been demonstrating more "concern and regard for safeguarding the rights and interests of the Muslims" than for "addressing even the basic necessities of the most marginalised section of Hindu society, the ‘untouchables’". In reducing the scope for communal disputes, Muslim secession would remove a constant distraction from the true democratic challenge of independence, the urgent need for social reforms.[[75]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-:42-76):225–248

Ambedkar accepted that, with compulsory population exchanges,[[76]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-77) the provincial boundaries of Punjab and Bengal should be redrawn to separate the Muslim and non-Muslim majority parts. Venkat Dhulipala states that such dissent from the all-India nationalist position "rocked Indian politics for a decade". It determined the course of dialogue between the Muslim League and the Indian National Congress, paving the way for a two-state solution.[[77]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-78)[[78]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-79) In his preface to a second, 1945, edition of _Pakistan or The Partition of India_, Ambedkar claimed that, as they edged toward agreement on [partition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_of_India "Partition of India"), Gandhi and [Muhammad Ali Jinnah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_Ali_Jinnah "Muhammad Ali Jinnah") had come to view his work as "an authority" on the subject.[[75]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-:42-76):11

## India's Constitution

[![Image 13](http://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/ad/Dr._Babasaheb_Ambedkar%2C_chairman_of_the_Drafting_Committee%2C_presenting_the_final_draft_of_the_Indian_Constitution_to_Dr._Rajendra_Prasad_on_25_November%2C_1949.jpg/250px-thumbnail.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Dr._Babasaheb_Ambedkar,_chairman_of_the_Drafting_Committee,_presenting_the_final_draft_of_the_Indian_Constitution_to_Dr._Rajendra_Prasad_on_25_November,_1949.jpg)

Ambedkar, chairman of the Drafting Committee, presenting the final draft of the Indian Constitution to Rajendra Prasad, president of the Constituent Assembly, on 25 November 1949

Upon India's independence on 15 August 1947, the new prime minister [Jawaharlal Nehru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jawaharlal_Nehru "Jawaharlal Nehru") invited Ambedkar to serve as the [Dominion of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominion_of_India "Dominion of India")'s [Law Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_Law_and_Justice "Minister of Law and Justice"); two weeks later, he was appointed Chairman of the Drafting Committee of the Constitution for the future [Republic of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_India "Republic of India").

On 25 November 1949, Ambedkar in his concluding speech in constituent assembly said:[[79]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-80)

> "The credit that is given to me does not really belong to me. It belongs partly to Sir [B.N. Rau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B.N._Rau "B.N. Rau") the Constitutional Advisor to the Constituent Assembly who prepared a rough draft of the Constitution for the consideration of the Drafting Committee."[[80]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-81)

Indian constitution guarantees and protections for a wide range of [civil liberties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_liberties "Civil liberties") for individual citizens, including freedom of religion, the abolition of untouchability, and the outlawing of all forms of discrimination. Ambedkar was one of the ministers who argued for extensive economic and social rights for women, and won the Assembly's support for introducing a system of [reservations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reservation_in_India "Reservation in India") of jobs in the civil services, schools and colleges for members of [scheduled castes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scheduled_Castes_and_Tribes "Scheduled Castes and Tribes") and [scheduled tribes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scheduled_tribe "Scheduled tribe") and [Other Backward Class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Other_Backward_Class "Other Backward Class"), a system akin to [affirmative action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affirmative_action "Affirmative action"). India's lawmakers hoped to eradicate the socio-economic inequalities and lack of opportunities for India's depressed classes through these measures.[[81]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-Sheth-82) The Constitution was adopted on 26 November 1949 by the Constituent Assembly.[[82]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-83)

[Steven Calabresi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Calabresi "Steven Calabresi") has voiced agreement with [Martha C. Nussbaum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martha_C._Nussbaum "Martha C. Nussbaum")'s assessment of the Indian constitution as "Ambedkar's constitution," on account of the [Supreme Court of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_India "Supreme Court of India") having evolved away from [judicial restraint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judicial_restraint "Judicial restraint") and [legal positivism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legal_positivism "Legal positivism") favored and successfully executed into the Constitution in 1950 by [Jawaharlal Nehru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jawaharlal_Nehru "Jawaharlal Nehru") and leaders of India's [anti-colonial nationalist movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_independence_movement "Indian independence movement") who held sway in the [Constituent Assembly of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constituent_Assembly_of_India "Constituent Assembly of India") to an [purposive approach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purposive_approach "Purposive approach") by which the court rules today, whereby it is the final arbiter of the Constitution's meaning and its interpretations are binding on the executive and legislative branches of the government. Ambedkar had supported the purposive approach during the drafting of the constitution from 1947 to 1949 but his views were in a minority.[[83]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-calabresi-union-of-India-84)

Ambedkar had openly shared his concern in the Constituent Assembly that country might lose both its Constitution and its democratic freedoms in the future. He believed that the lack of a strong democratic tradition, the widespread presence of ‘bhakti’ or political ‘hero worship’ and the large gap between the republican promises of the Constitution and socio-economic inequality, made this a distinct and ever-present possibility.[[84]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-85)[[85]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-86)[[86]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-87)

Ambedkar expressed his disapproval for the constitution in 1953 during a parliament session and said "People always keep on saying to me "Oh you are the maker of the constitution". My answer is I was a hack. What I was asked to do, I did much against my will." Ambedkar added that, "I am quite prepared to say that I shall be the first person to burn it out. I do not want it. It does not suit anybody."[[87]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-88)[[88]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-89)

## Economics

Ambedkar was the first Indian to pursue a doctorate in economics abroad.[[89]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-IEA-90) He argued that industrialisation and agricultural growth could enhance the Indian economy.[[90]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-Mishra-91) He stressed investment in agriculture as the primary industry of India.[_[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation\_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_] Ambedkar advocated national economic and social development, stressing education, public hygiene, community health, residential facilities as the basic amenities.[[90]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-Mishra-91) His DSc thesis, _The problem of the Rupee: Its Origin and Solution_ (1923) examines the causes for the Rupee's fall in value. In this dissertation, he argued in favour of a gold standard in modified form, and was opposed to the gold-exchange standard favoured by Keynes in his treatise _Indian Currency and Finance_ (1909), claiming it was less stable. He favoured the stoppage of all further coinage of the rupee and the minting of a gold coin, which he believed would fix currency rates and prices.[[91]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-Jadhav-1991-92)

He also analysed revenue in his PhD dissertation _The Evolution of Provincial Finance in British India_. In this work, he analysed the various systems used by the British colonial government to manage finances in India.[[91]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-Jadhav-1991-92)[[92]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-Zelliot_Ambedkar_and_America-93) His views on finance were that governments should ensure their expenditures have "faithfulness, wisdom and economy." "Faithfulness" meaning governments should use money as nearly as possible to the original intentions of spending the money in the first place. "Wisdom" meaning it should be used as well as possible for the public good, and "economy" meaning the funds should be used so that the maximum value can be extracted from them.[[93]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-94)

Ambedkar opposed income tax for low-income groups. He contributed in Land Revenue Tax and excise duty policies to stabilise the economy.[_[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation\_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_] He played an important role in land reform and the state economic development.[_[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation\_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_] According to him, the caste system, due to its division of labourers and hierarchical nature, impedes movement of labour (higher castes would not do lower-caste occupations) and movement of capital (assuming investors would invest first in their own caste occupation). His theory of State Socialism had three points: state ownership of agricultural land, the maintenance of resources for production by the state, and a just distribution of these resources to the population. He emphasised a free economy with a stable Rupee which India has adopted recently.[_[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation\_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_] He advocated birth control to develop the Indian economy, and this has been adopted by Indian government as national policy for family planning. He emphasised equal rights for women for economic development.[_[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation\_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_]

A number of Ambedkar's ideas reflected deep interest in [Austrian school of economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_school_of_economics "Austrian school of economics"). The ideas of Ambedkar were close to those of [Carl Menger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Menger "Carl Menger"), [Ludwig von Mises](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig_von_Mises "Ludwig von Mises"), [Friedrich Hayek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich_Hayek "Friedrich Hayek"), and [William Graham Sumner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Graham_Sumner "William Graham Sumner"). Ambedkar's theory of free banking was built on Menger's work and also on [Gopal Krishna Gokhale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gopal_Krishna_Gokhale "Gopal Krishna Gokhale")'s treatise on finance and money. Ambedkar's view about distinguishing differential quality of money was influenced by Menger's idea of sale-ability of money which is found in Menger's article 'On the Origin of Money'. Ambedkar's recommendations for free banking were ignored by both Royal Commission and Indian government.[[94]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-Barnett-95)

In his book, "The Evolution of Provincial Finance in British India", Ambedkar wrote "a Central Government for the whole of India could not be said to possess knowledge and experience of all various conditions prevailing in different Provinces under it. It, therefore, necessarily becomes an authority less competent to deal with matters of provincial administration than the Provisional Governments.'[[94]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-Barnett-95)

Ambedkar's views on agricultural land was that too much of it was idle, or that it was not being utilized properly. He believed there was an "ideal proportion" of production factors that would allow agricultural land to be used most productively. To this end, he saw the large portion of people who lived on agriculture at the time as a major problem. Therefore, he advocated industrialization of the economy to allow these agricultural labourers to be of more use elsewhere.[_[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation\_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_] Ambedkar was of the view that there is a need to shift surplus labour from agricultural channels to non-agricultural channels.[[95]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-Kattel-96)

Ambedkar was trained as an economist, and was a professional economist until 1921, when he became a political leader. He wrote three books on economics:

*   _Administration and Finance of the East India Company_
*   _The Evolution of Provincial Finance in British India_
*   _The Problem of the Rupee: Its Origin and Its Solution_[[96]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-autogenerated3-97)[[97]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-autogenerated1-98)

## Marriage

[![Image 14](http://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/cf/Dr._B.R._Ambedkar_with_wife_Dr._Savita_Ambedkar_in_1948.jpg/250px-Dr._B.R._Ambedkar_with_wife_Dr._Savita_Ambedkar_in_1948.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Dr._B.R._Ambedkar_with_wife_Dr._Savita_Ambedkar_in_1948.jpg)

Ambedkar with wife [Savita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savita_Ambedkar "Savita Ambedkar") in 1948

Ambedkar's first wife [Ramabai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramabai_Bhimrao_Ambedkar "Ramabai Bhimrao Ambedkar") died in 1935 after prolonged illness. After completing the draft of India's constitution in the late 1940s, he suffered from lack of sleep, had [neuropathic pain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuropathic_pain "Neuropathic pain") in his legs, and was taking [insulin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insulin "Insulin") and [homoeopathic medicines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeopathy "Homeopathy"). He went to Bombay for treatment, and there met [Sharada Kabir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savita_Ambedkar "Savita Ambedkar"), [MBBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MBBS "MBBS"), whom he married on 15 April 1948, at his home in New Delhi. Doctors recommended a companion who was a good cook and was a medical practitioner to care for him.[[98]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-99) She adopted the name [Savita Ambedkar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savita_Ambedkar "Savita Ambedkar") and cared for him the rest of his life.[[99]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-Columbia6-100) Savita Ambedkar, who was called also 'Mai', died on 29 May 2003, aged 93 in Mumbai.[[100]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-101)

## Conversion to Buddhism

[![Image 15](http://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/fc/Dr._Ambedkar_delivering_speech_during_conversion.jpg/250px-Dr._Ambedkar_delivering_speech_during_conversion.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Dr._Ambedkar_delivering_speech_during_conversion.jpg)

Ambedkar delivering a speech during a mass conversion ceremony

Ambedkar considered converting to [Sikhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikhism "Sikhism"), which encouraged opposition to oppression and so appealed to leaders of scheduled castes. But after meeting with Sikh leaders, he concluded that he might get "second-rate" Sikh status.[[101]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-102)

Instead, around 1950, he began devoting his attention to Buddhism and travelled to [Ceylon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominion_of_Ceylon "Dominion of Ceylon") (now Sri Lanka) to attend a meeting of the [World Fellowship of Buddhists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Fellowship_of_Buddhists "World Fellowship of Buddhists").[[102]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-Sanghara_kshita-103) While dedicating a new Buddhist [vihara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vihara "Vihara") near [Pune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pune "Pune"), Ambedkar announced he was writing a book on Buddhism, and that when it was finished, he would formally convert to Buddhism.[[103]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-Columbia7-104) He twice visited Burma in 1954; the second time to attend the third conference of the World Fellowship of Buddhists in [Rangoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rangoon "Rangoon").[[104]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-Docker-105) In 1955, he founded the Bharatiya Bauddha Mahasabha, or the Buddhist Society of India.[[105]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-Quack-106) In 1956, he completed his final work, _[The Buddha and His Dhamma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_Buddha\_and\_His\_Dhamma "The Buddha and His Dhamma")_, and requested Jawaharlal Nehru to help him promote the book through government funding. Nehru rejected Ambedkar's request.[[106]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-z247-107) The book was later published posthumously.[[105]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-Quack-106)

After meetings with the Sri Lankan Buddhist monk [Hammalawa Saddhatissa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hammalawa_Saddhatissa "Hammalawa Saddhatissa"),[[107]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-108) Ambedkar organised a formal public ceremony for himself and his supporters in [Nagpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagpur "Nagpur") on 14 October 1956. Accepting the [Three Refuges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Jewels "Three Jewels") and [Five Precepts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Five_Precepts "Five Precepts") from a Buddhist [monk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhikkhu "Bhikkhu") in the traditional manner, Ambedkar completed his own conversion, along with his wife. He then proceeded to convert some 500,000 of his supporters who were gathered around him.[[103]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-Columbia7-104)[[108]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-109) He prescribed the [22 Vows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/22_Vows "22 Vows") for these converts, after the Three Jewels and Five Precepts. He then travelled to [Kathmandu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kathmandu "Kathmandu"), Nepal to attend the Fourth World Buddhist Conference.[[104]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-Docker-105) His work on _The Buddha or Karl Marx_ and "Revolution and counter-revolution in ancient India" remained incomplete.[_[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation\_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_]

## Death

[![Image 16](http://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/62/Dr._Babasaheb_Ambedkar_as_he_passed_away_in_his_sleep_at_his_residence_26%2C_Alipore_Road%2C_New_Delhi_on_6_December_1956.jpg/250px-Dr._Babasaheb_Ambedkar_as_he_passed_away_in_his_sleep_at_his_residence_26%2C_Alipore_Road%2C_New_Delhi_on_6_December_1956.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Dr._Babasaheb_Ambedkar_as_he_passed_away_in_his_sleep_at_his_residence_26,_Alipore_Road,_New_Delhi_on_6_December_1956.jpg)

Death of B. R. Ambedkar

Since 1948, Ambedkar had [diabetes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diabetes "Diabetes"). He remained in bed from June to October in 1954 due to medication side-effects and poor eyesight.[[103]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-Columbia7-104) His health worsened during 1955. Three days after completing his final manuscript _[The Buddha and His Dhamma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The\_Buddha\_and\_His\_Dhamma "The Buddha and His Dhamma")_, Ambedkar died in his sleep on 6 December 1956 at his home in Delhi.[[109]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-110)

A Buddhist cremation was organised at Dadar [Chowpatty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chowpatty "Chowpatty") beach on 7 December,[[110]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-Sangharakshita_2006_162%E2%80%93163-111) attended by half a million grieving people.[[111]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-112) No ministers attended his funeral.[[112]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-d510-113) A conversion program was organised on 16 December 1956,[[113]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-Detlef_Kantowsky_2003-114) so that cremation attendees were also converted to Buddhism at the same place.[[113]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-Detlef_Kantowsky_2003-114)

Ambedkar was survived by his second wife [Savita Ambedkar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savita_Ambedkar "Savita Ambedkar") (known as Maisaheb Ambedkar), who died in 2003,[[114]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-115) and his son [Yashwant Ambedkar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yashwant_Ambedkar "Yashwant Ambedkar") (known as Bhaiyasaheb Ambedkar), who died in 1977.[[115]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-116) Savita and Yashwant carried on the socio-religious movement started by B. R. Ambedkar. Yashwant served as the 2nd President of the [Buddhist Society of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhist_Society_of_India "Buddhist Society of India") (1957–1977) and a member of the [Maharashtra Legislative Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maharashtra_Legislative_Council "Maharashtra Legislative Council") (1960–1966).[[116]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-117)[[117]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-118) Ambedkar's elder grandson, [Prakash Yashwant Ambedkar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prakash_Yashwant_Ambedkar "Prakash Yashwant Ambedkar"), is the chief-adviser of the Buddhist Society of India,[[118]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-119) leads the [Vanchit Bahujan Aghadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanchit_Bahujan_Aghadi "Vanchit Bahujan Aghadi")[[119]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-120)[[120]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-parliamentofindia.nic.in-121) and has served in both houses of the [Indian Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Parliament "Indian Parliament").[[120]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-parliamentofindia.nic.in-121) Ambedkar's younger grandson, [Anandraj Ambedkar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anandraj_Ambedkar "Anandraj Ambedkar") leads the Republican Sena (tran: The "Republican Army").[[121]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-122)

A number of unfinished typescripts and handwritten drafts were found among Ambedkar's notes and papers and gradually made available. Among these were _[Waiting for a Visa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waiting\_for\_a\_Visa "Waiting for a Visa")_, which probably dates from 1935 to 1936 and is an autobiographical work, and the _Untouchables, or the Children of India's Ghetto_, which refers to the census of 1951.[[103]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-Columbia7-104)

A memorial for Ambedkar was established in his Delhi house at [26 Alipur Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/26_Alipur_Road "26 Alipur Road"). His birthdate known as _[Ambedkar Jayanti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambedkar\_Jayanti "Ambedkar Jayanti")_ or _Bhim Jayanti_ is observed as a public holiday in many Indian states. He was posthumously awarded India's highest civilian honour, the [Bharat Ratna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bharat_Ratna "Bharat Ratna"), in 1990.[[122]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-123)

On the anniversary of his birth and death, and on [Dhamma Chakra Pravartan Din](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhamma_Chakra_Pravartan_Din "Dhamma Chakra Pravartan Din") (14 October) at Nagpur, at least half a million people gather to pay homage to him at his memorial in Mumbai.[[123]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-124) Thousands of bookshops are set up, and books are sold. His message to his followers was "educate, agitate, organise!"[[124]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-125)

## Legacy

[![Image 17](http://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f0/People_paying_tribute_at_the_central_statue_of_Bodhisattva_Babasaheb_Ambedkar_in_Dr._Babasaheb_Ambedkar_Marathwada_University%2C_India.png/250px-People_paying_tribute_at_the_central_statue_of_Bodhisattva_Babasaheb_Ambedkar_in_Dr._Babasaheb_Ambedkar_Marathwada_University%2C_India.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:People_paying_tribute_at_the_central_statue_of_Bodhisattva_Babasaheb_Ambedkar_in_Dr._Babasaheb_Ambedkar_Marathwada_University,_India.png)

People paying tribute at the central statue of Ambedkar in [Dr. Babasaheb Ambedkar Marathwada University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr._Babasaheb_Ambedkar_Marathwada_University "Dr. Babasaheb Ambedkar Marathwada University") in [Aurangabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aurangabad,_Maharashtra "Aurangabad, Maharashtra").

[![Image 18](http://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/5b/1_RUPEE_DR._B.R_AMBEDKAR_CENTENARY_1990_COMMEMORATIVE_COIN.jpg/250px-1_RUPEE_DR._B.R_AMBEDKAR_CENTENARY_1990_COMMEMORATIVE_COIN.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:1_RUPEE_DR._B.R_AMBEDKAR_CENTENARY_1990_COMMEMORATIVE_COIN.jpg)

1990 [1 Rupee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_1-rupee_coin "Indian 1-rupee coin")[commemorative coin of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coins_of_the_Indian_rupee#Commemorative_coins "Coins of the Indian rupee") dedicated to B.R. Ambedkar

Decades after death of Ambedkar, his relevance increased with Indian politics getting competitive and divisive.[[125]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-c604-126)[[126]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-t474-127) Scholar [Anand Teltumbde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anand_Teltumbde "Anand Teltumbde") noted, "Earlier when there was a monopoly of [the Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_National_Congress "Indian National Congress"), he was neglected. Even 10 years after his death in 1956, there was not a single road or a monument in his name."[[125]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-c604-126) Ambedkar's image as a socio-political reformer saw a deep effect on modern India.[[127]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-128)[[128]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-129) In post-Independence India, his socio-political thought is respected across the political spectrum. His initiatives have influenced various spheres of life and transformed the way India today looks at socio-economic policies, education and [affirmative action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affirmative_action "Affirmative action") through socio-economic and legal incentives. He passionately believed in individual freedom and criticised caste society. His accusations of [Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism "Hinduism") as being the foundation of the caste system made him controversial and unpopular among Hindus.[[129]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-130) His conversion to Buddhism sparked a revival in interest in Buddhist philosophy in India and abroad.[[130]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-131)

Many public institutions are named in his honour, and the [Dr. Babasaheb Ambedkar International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr._Babasaheb_Ambedkar_International_Airport "Dr. Babasaheb Ambedkar International Airport") in [Nagpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagpur "Nagpur"), otherwise known as [Sonegaon Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonegaon_Airport "Sonegaon Airport"). [Dr. B. R. Ambedkar National Institute of Technology, Jalandhar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr._B._R._Ambedkar_National_Institute_of_Technology,_Jalandhar "Dr. B. R. Ambedkar National Institute of Technology, Jalandhar"), [Ambedkar University Delhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambedkar_University_Delhi "Ambedkar University Delhi") is also named in his honour.[[131]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-132)

The Maharashtra government has acquired a house in London where Ambedkar lived during his days as a student in the 1920s. The house is expected to be converted into a museum-cum-memorial to Ambedkar.[[132]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-133)

Ambedkar was voted "[the Greatest Indian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Greatest_Indian "The Greatest Indian")" since independence by a poll organised by [History TV18](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_TV18 "History TV18") and [CNN IBN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CNN_IBN "CNN IBN"), ahead of Patel and Nehru, in 2012. Nearly 20 million votes were cast.[[133]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-outlookindia.com-134) Due to his role in economics, [Narendra Jadhav](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narendra_Jadhav "Narendra Jadhav"), a notable Indian economist,[[134]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-Planning_Commission-135) has said that Ambedkar was "the highest educated Indian economist of all times."[[135]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-PISHAROTY-136)[Amartya Sen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amartya_Sen "Amartya Sen"), said that Ambedkar is "father of my economics", and "he was highly controversial figure in his home country, though it was not the reality. His contribution in the field of economics is marvelous and will be remembered forever."[[136]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-137)[[137]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-138)

On 2 April 1967, an 3.66 metre (12 foot) tall bronze statue of Ambedkar was installed in the [Parliament of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_India "Parliament of India"). The statue, sculpted by B.V. Wagh, was unveiled by the then President of India, [Sarvepalli Radhakrishnan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarvepalli_Radhakrishnan "Sarvepalli Radhakrishnan").[[138]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-RS-statue-139)[[139]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-140)[[140]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-141) On 12 April 1990, a portrait of B.R. Ambedkar is put in the Central Hall of Parliament House.[[141]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-rajyasabha.nic.in-142)[[142]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-143)[[143]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-144) The portrait of Ambedkar, painted by Zeba Amrohawi, was unveiled by the then Prime Minister of India, [V. P. Singh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V._P._Singh "V. P. Singh").[[141]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-rajyasabha.nic.in-142) Another portrait of Ambedkar is put in the Parliamentary Museum and archives of the Parliament House.[[144]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-145)[[145]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-146)

Ambedkar's legacy was not without criticism. Ambedkar has been criticised for his one-sided views on the issue of caste at the expense of cooperation with the larger nationalist movement.[[146]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-147) Ambedkar has been also criticised by some of his biographers over his neglect of organization-building.[[147]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-148)

Ambedkar's political philosophy has given rise to a large number of political parties, publications and workers' unions that remain active across India, especially in [Maharashtra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maharashtra "Maharashtra"). His promotion of Buddhism has rejuvenated interest in Buddhist philosophy among sections of population in India. Mass conversion ceremonies have been organised by human rights activists in modern times, emulating Ambedkar's Nagpur ceremony of 1956.[[148]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-149) Some Indian Buddhists regard him as a [Bodhisattva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bodhisattva "Bodhisattva"), although he never claimed it himself.[[149]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-150) Outside India, during the late 1990s, some Hungarian [Romani people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romani_people "Romani people") drew parallels between their own situation and that of the downtrodden people in India. Inspired by Ambedkar, they started to convert to Buddhism.[[150]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-151)

The Ambedkar Statue in [Hyderabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyderabad "Hyderabad") is a statue of B. R. Ambedkar located in Hyderabad. The statue was designed by [Ram V. Sutar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ram_V._Sutar "Ram V. Sutar"). The foundation stone was laid in 2016, but the construction of the statue began in 2021. The statue was inaugurated on 14 April 2023, by [K. Chandrashekhar Rao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K._Chandrashekar_Rao "K. Chandrashekar Rao"), the Chief Minister of Telangana, on the 132nd [Ambedkar Jayanti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambedkar_Jayanti "Ambedkar Jayanti"). Ambedkar's grandson [Prakash Ambedkar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prakash_Yashwant_Ambedkar "Prakash Yashwant Ambedkar") was the chief guest of the event. The statue is made up of over 360 tonnes of steel and 100 tonnes of bronze.[[151]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-152)[[152]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-153)[[153]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-154)[[154]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-155)[[155]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-156) On 19 January 2024, a 125 feet tall "[Statue of Social Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statue_of_Social_Justice "Statue of Social Justice")" of Ambedkar was installed in [Vijayawada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vijayawada "Vijayawada"), Andhra Pradesh, which stands on an 81 feet high platform.[[156]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-157) The Ambedkar statues in Hyderabad and Vijayawada are the fifth and fourth [tallest statues in India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tallest_statues_in_India "Tallest statues in India") respectively. In May 2026, a 450 feet tall "[Statue of Equality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statue_of_Equality_(Ambedkar) "Statue of Equality (Ambedkar)")" of Babasaheb Ambedkar will be ready at Indu Mill in [Mumbai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mumbai "Mumbai"),[[157]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-158) which will be the second tallest statue in India and the third tallest in the world.[[158]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-159)

## Views

### Religion

[![Image 19](http://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d0/47_Raika_School_-_eating_together_%283384824242%29.jpg/250px-47_Raika_School_-_eating_together_%283384824242%29.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:47_Raika_School_-_eating_together_(3384824242).jpg)

Children awaiting school lunch in Rayka (also Raika), a village in rural Gujarat. The salutation _[Jai Bhim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jai\_Bhim "Jai Bhim")_ written on the blackboard honours Ambedkar.

Ambedkar said in 1935 that he was born a Hindu but would not die a Hindu. He viewed Hinduism as an "oppressive religion" and started to consider conversion to any other religion.[[159]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-Rao1999-160) In _Annihilation of Caste_, Ambedkar claims that the only lasting way a true casteless society could be achieved is through destroying the belief of the sanctity of the _Shastras_ and denying their authority.[[160]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-161) Ambedkar was critical of Hindu religious texts and epics and wrote a work titled _[Riddles in Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riddles\_in\_Hinduism "Riddles in Hinduism")_ during 1954–1955. The work was published posthumously by combining individual chapter manuscripts and resulted in mass demonstrations and counter demonstrations.[[161]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-162)[[162]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-163)[[163]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-164)

Ambedkar viewed Christianity to be incapable of fighting injustices. He wrote that "It is an incontrovertible fact that Christianity was not enough to end the slavery of the Negroes in the United States. A civil war was necessary to give the Negro the freedom which was denied to him by the Christians."[[164]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-165)

Ambedkar criticized distinctions within Islam and described the religion as "a close corporation and the distinction that it makes between Muslims and non-Muslims is a very real, very positive and very alienating distinction".[[165]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-RoyBecker2020-166)

He opposed conversions of depressed classes to convert to Islam or Christianity added that if they converted to Islam then "the danger of Muslim domination also becomes real" and if they converted to Christianity then it "will help to strengthen the hold of Britain on the country".[[166]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-Keer1971-167)

Initially, Ambedkar planned to convert to Sikhism but he rejected this idea after he discovered that British government would not guarantee the privileges accorded to the untouchables in reserved parliamentary seats.[[167]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-Oddie1991-168)

On 16 October 1956, he converted to Buddhism just weeks before his death.[[168]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-Viswanathan2021-169)

### Indo-Aryan migrations

Ambedkar viewed the [Shudras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shudras "Shudras") as Aryan and rejected the [Indo-Aryan migrations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Aryan_migrations "Indo-Aryan migrations"), describing it as "so absurd that it ought to have been dead long ago" in his 1946 book _[Who Were the Shudras?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Who\_Were\_the\_Shudras%3F "Who Were the Shudras?")_.[[169]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-Bryant,_Edwin_2001._pp._50-51-170) Ambedkar viewed Shudras as originally having been "part of the Kshatriya Varna in the Indo-Aryan society", but became socially degraded after they inflicted many tyrannies on [Brahmins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahmins "Brahmins").[[170]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-171)

Ambedkar disputed various hypotheses of the Aryan homeland being [outside India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Aryan_migration_theory "Indo-Aryan migration theory"), and concluded the Aryan homeland was India itself. According to Ambedkar, the Rig Veda says Aryans, Dāsa and Dasyus were competing religious groups, not different peoples.[[171]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-172)

### Communism

Ambedkar was heavily influenced by [John Dewey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Dewey "John Dewey") and his philosophy of [pragmatism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pragmatism "Pragmatism").[[7]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-epw.in-8)[[8]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-Skaria_450%E2%80%93465-9) Ambedkar's views on [Communism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communism "Communism") were expressed in two 1956 texts, "Buddha or Karl Marx" and "Buddhism and Communism".[[172]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-173) According to Jaffrelot, Ambedkar "constantly criticised the Communists, whom he accused of exploiting the workers' cause to advance their own career".[[58]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-Jaffrelot_2005_76%E2%80%9377-59) In a 1938 speech delivered to the Masur conference of depressed classes, Ambedkar said: "It is absolutely impossible for me to keep relations with the communists. I am an implacable enemy of the Communists."[[173]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-174)

Ambedkar stated that:[[174]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-175)

> Carlyle called political economy a pig philosophy. Carlyle was of course wrong. For man needs material comforts. But the communist philosophy seems to equally wrong, for the aim of their philosophy seems to be fatten pigs, as though men are no better than pigs. Man must grow materially as well as spiritually. Buddha’s method was to change the mind of man without the use of force. Buddha sought to change man’s moral disposition to follow the path voluntarily.

He accepted that the privileged few's [exploitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exploitation_of_labour "Exploitation of labour") of the masses perpetuated poverty and its issues. However, he did not see this exploitation as purely economic, theorizing that the cultural aspects of exploitation are as bad or worse than economic exploitation. In addition, he did not see economic relationships as the only important aspect of human life. He identified that "communism stands for revolution and not for reformation" and also saw Communists as willing to resort to any means to achieve [proletarian revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proletarian_revolution "Proletarian revolution"), including violence, while he himself saw democratic and peaceful measures as the best option for change. Ambedkar also opposed the Marxist idea of controlling all the [means of production](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Means_of_production "Means of production") and ending private ownership of property: seeing the latter measure as not able to fix the problems of society. In addition, Ambedkar rejected that state was a temporary institution and did not subscribe to the Marxian theory that [it would eventually wither away](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Withering_away_of_the_state "Withering away of the state"), Ambedkar believed in a classless society, but also believed the state would exist as long as society and that it should be active in development.[[91]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-Jadhav-1991-92) But in the 1950s, in an interview he gave to BBC, he accepted that the current liberal democratic system will collapse and the alternative, as he thinks, "is some kind of communism".[[175]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-176) Marxist theoretician Abhinav Sinha argues that Ambedkar was actually "warning the establishment about the possibility of communist revolution" rather than advocating for it.[[176]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-Anvil-177)

Ambedkar's objections to communism were severely criticized by Marxist theoretician Abhinav Sinha, writing for the magazine _The Anvil_, he notes:[[176]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-Anvil-177)

> "Communism is not a sect of Bhikkhus and Ambedkar did not understand this simple fact, in his zealous attempt to prove that everything correct in Marxism has already been said by Buddha and whatever was not said by Buddha is incorrect in Marxism. However, it seems that Ambedkar made these bold claims without reading a single Marxist text. He also claims that a 60 year old political philosophy like communism would not work. And what would work? A 2500 years old philosophy of Buddhism! These are some of the “refutations” of Marxism by Ambedkar!" .... "Ambedkar did not have any understanding about Marxism as he had not read a single work by Marx, Engels or Lenin. Therefore the very question whether Ambedkar agreed or disagreed, in real sense of the terms, with Marxism or not is a non-question. The truth is that he did not have any understanding of Marxism."

## In popular culture

Several films, plays, and other works have been based on the life and thoughts of Ambedkar.

*   Indian director [Jabbar Patel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jabbar_Patel "Jabbar Patel") made a documentary titled _Dr. Babasaheb Ambedkar_ in 1991; he followed this with a full-length feature film _[Dr. Babasaheb Ambedkar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr.\_Babasaheb\_Ambedkar\_(film) "Dr. Babasaheb Ambedkar (film)")_ in 2000 with [Mammootty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mammootty "Mammootty") in the lead role.[[177]](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_note-178) This biopic was sponsored by the [National Film Development Corporation of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Film_Development_Corporation_of_India "National Film Development Corporation of India") and the government's [Ministry of Social Justice and Empowerment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Social_Justice_and_Empowerment "Ministry of Social Justice and Empowerment"). [ISBN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISBN_(identifier) "ISBN (identifier)")[978-0-19-949144-5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special:BookSources/978-0-19-949144-5 "Special:BookSources/978-0-19-949144-5"). In his role as Constitutional Adviser, Rau's contributions consisted of not only advising the Constituent Assembly but also members of the Indian National Congress, and the All-India Muslim League. In the Constituent Assembly itself, Rau's influence was substantive. Significantly, Rau produced the draft constitution, which the drafting committee chaired by B. R. Ambedkar went on to deliberate. Subsequently, Rau was involved in evaluating the comments received on the draft constitution and incorporating them before it was submitted to the Constituent Assembly in late 1948.
3.   [↑](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_ref-cewing-rau-rights-2020-lead1_4-0)Ewing, Cindy (2020). "Codifying Minority Rights: Postcolonial Constitutionalism in Burma, Ceylon, and India". In Moses, A. Dirk; Duranti, Marco; Burke, Roland (eds.). _Decolonization, Self-Determination, and the rise of Global Human Rights Politics_. Cambridge University Press. pp.179–206. [ISBN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISBN_(identifier) "ISBN (identifier)")[978-1-108-47935-6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special:BookSources/978-1-108-47935-6 "Special:BookSources/978-1-108-47935-6"). (p. 190) In order to protect both individual and group rights, India's constitutional adviser B. N. Rau prepared a key brief that changed how nights were expressed and enumerated in the constitution. His September 1946 note on fundamental rights expounded on justiciability and the need to identify which rights would be enforceable in court. Justiciability thus became a way to bridge the gap between committee members who wanted to write in as many rights as possible and those who wanted a more narrow set of rights. Rau laid out a scheme to divide fundamental rights into two categories: legally enforceable and not enforceable, pointing to the Irish constitution as a model for framing "a distinction between two broad classes of rights," referring to "certain rights which require positive action by the State and which can be guaranteed only so far as such action is practicable, while others merely require that the State shall abstain from prejudicial action." The fundamental rights subcommittee debated Rau's proposal in February 1947, with committee members K. M. Munshi and B. R. Ambedkar initially opposing splitting these rights. ... (p. 191) In March 1947, after the subcommittee concluded debate, the assembly agreed to the expansive bill of rights and divided fundamental rights into two sections based on Rau's original proposal. ... (pp. 192–193) In early April 1947, Rau completed the fundamental rights report. The constituent assembly convened for its third session later that month and each subcommittee presented its findings over a series of raucous debates. Patel presented the new draft bill of rights, which was adopted by the assembly on August 28, 1947. Observing these sessions at Constitution House was Chan Htoon, the Burmese constitutional adviser. Aung San tasked Chan Htoon with studying the constitutions of different countries and developing relationships with members of the Indian legal academy. Having developed a friendship with Rau, Chan Htoon discussed the draft constitution with Rau in the setting of the constituent assembly. The cross-border consultations between India and Burma led to the intertwining of their constitutional ideas, best exemplified by their provisions on individual rights and state duties.
4.   [↑](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_ref-prasse-freeman-2023-lead_5-0)Prasse-Freeman, Elliott (2023). Cindy Ewing reveals that the statements in Burma's constitution about citizen rights were imported wholesale from other sources—either India or Ireland (2020, 193). The section on fundamental rights copies word for word from the Advisory Committee of the Indian Constituent Assembly. This mimicry was not coincidental. Chan Htoon, the author of Burma's constitution, "developed a friendship with [B. N.] Rau," the author of India's constitution, when visiting New Delhi "to observe [India's] constitutional debates." While there, "Rau reviewed Chan Htoon's draft constitution and assisted in collecting materials for the Burmese constituent assembly. Rau ensured significant areas of commonality between the two constitutions" (Ewing 2020, 193).

6.   [↑](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._R._Ambedkar#cite_ref-britannica-ambedkar_7-0)["B. R. Ambedkar Indian Political Leader"](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Bhimrao-Ramji-Ambedkar). _Encyclopaedia Britannica_. Bhimrao Ramji Ambedkar (born April 14, 1891, Mhow, India—died December 6, 1956, New Delhi) was a leader of the Dalits (Scheduled Castes; formerly called untouchables) and law minister of the government of India (1947–51). In 1947 Ambedkar became the law minister of the government of India. He took a leading part in the framing of the Indian constitution, outlawing discrimination against untouchables, and skillfully helped to steer it through the assembly; the adoption of the constitution on January 26, 1950, is today celebrated as Republic Day, a national holiday. He resigned in 1951, disappointed at his lack of influence in the government. In October 1956, in despair because of the perpetuation of untouchability in Hindu doctrine, he renounced Hinduism and became a Buddhist, together with about 200,000 fellow Dalits, at a ceremony in Nagpur.
